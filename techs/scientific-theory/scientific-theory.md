# Scientific Theory (and the Scientific Method)

**Formalized in Baghdad ~1000 CE by Ibn al-Haytham. Adopted in Europe ~1600 CE by Galileo, Newton, and others. Still the best way humans have found to figure out what's true.**

Before the scientific method, if you wanted to know something about the world, you mostly had a few options: **ask a priest, ask a philosopher, read an old book, or guess.** These didn't work very well. Priests told you what the gods thought. Philosophers reasoned from first principles that were often wrong. Old books were full of things Aristotle had made up and everyone had been repeating for 1,000 years.

## Ibn al-Haytham (~1000 CE) invented a better way

An Iraqi scholar named **Ibn al-Haytham** (Latinized as *Alhazen*) was locked up under house arrest in Cairo by an unstable caliph. He used the time to design experiments — real, physical, repeatable experiments — to test how light works. He built early camera obscuras. He measured. He published his results in a book called the ***Book of Optics*** (*Kitab al-Manazir*) around 1015 CE.

The important thing about what Ibn al-Haytham did: **he wrote down exactly how he did his experiments so anyone else could repeat them and check.**

That is the scientific method. Everything modern science does still runs on that idea: don't just claim something is true — describe your experiment, describe your data, and let other people check.

Muslim scholars refined the method for the next few hundred years. Then Europe caught up.

## Europe finally figures it out (~1600 CE)

Ibn al-Haytham's *Book of Optics* was translated into Latin around 1200 CE and became a standard European university text. Roger Bacon, an English monk in the 1200s, was reading it and calling for a "science of experiments" — 400 years before it took hold in Europe.

The big European names most Americans learn about:

- **Galileo Galilei** (Italy, ~1600). Pointed a telescope at the moon and saw craters. Pointed it at Jupiter and saw moons orbiting it — proving that not everything in the sky orbits Earth. Wrote it down as data. The Church put him on house arrest for it.
- **Francis Bacon** (England, ~1620). Wrote *Novum Organum*, arguing that science must be based on experiments, not old books. Basically re-branded Ibn al-Haytham's method as "the scientific method."
- **Isaac Newton** (England, ~1687). Wrote the *Principia Mathematica*, laying out the laws of motion and gravity in mathematical form. Newton personally cited Ibn al-Haytham as an influence on his optics work.

By 1700, science in Europe had become a competitive, cross-country, publish-your-results-and-let-others-check-them enterprise. And it's basically been that ever since — spread now to every country in the world, run by researchers of every background.

## What the scientific method actually is

- Notice something.
- Make a **hypothesis** — an educated guess about what's going on.
- Design an **experiment** you can actually do that would tell you whether your hypothesis is right or wrong.
- Do the experiment.
- **Write down exactly what you did**, in enough detail that a stranger could repeat it.
- Publish your results.
- Let other scientists try to repeat what you did. If they can't repeat it, your result probably isn't real. If they can, it becomes part of scientific knowledge.

That's it. Not a religion. Not a set of beliefs. A **procedure**.

## What humanity kept

Every piece of technology built since 1700 comes from the scientific method — medicine, engineering, chemistry, biology, physics, computers, spacecraft, all of it. Every year humans live longer now than they did in 1700 comes partly from science (vaccines, antibiotics, sanitation, safer food). Every mode of transportation faster than a horse comes from science.

The scientific method is why humans went from a global population of about 600 million in 1700 to 8 billion today. It fed everyone. It also made a bunch of new problems (climate change, mass extinctions, nuclear weapons) that we're now trying to use the same method to fix.

## The honest, complicated part

Science is not neutral. Scientists are people. Governments and companies fund the science that fits their goals. Historically, some of the most horrible things humans have done to each other — eugenics, human experiments on prisoners, tobacco companies suppressing cancer research — used science or pretended to.

That doesn't make the method wrong. It means humans have to keep the method honest by being honest about what science is being used for and who profits from it.

Also: **science does not tell you what to value.** Science can tell you *how* something works. It can't tell you *whether it's a good idea*. That's a job for the rest of you — for ethics, for democracy, for religion, for community. Science is a tool, not a compass.
