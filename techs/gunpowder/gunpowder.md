# Gunpowder

**Invented in China around 850 CE, by alchemists searching for the elixir of life. Weaponized in China around 1000 CE. Reached Europe around 1250 CE. Rewrote warfare and, later, the political map of the world.**

Here is one of history's great ironies: **gunpowder was invented by scientists trying to make people live forever.**

Chinese Taoist alchemists in the 800s CE were mixing all kinds of natural ingredients trying to concoct a **potion of immortality**. One combination — **saltpeter (potassium nitrate), charcoal, and sulfur** — turned out to be the deadliest chemical they had ever mixed. It exploded.

They named it *huǒ yào* — literally "**fire medicine**." That's what "gunpowder" is called in Chinese to this day.

## From "fire medicine" to weapons

By around **1000 CE**, during the Song dynasty (see our [China page](../../civs/china/)), Chinese engineers had figured out that if you couldn't make people live forever, you could at least use fire medicine to kill your enemies more efficiently.

The Song military invented:

- **Fire arrows** — arrows with small gunpowder packets that exploded on impact
- **The fire lance** — a bamboo tube filled with gunpowder that a soldier could hold, essentially an early flamethrower
- **Bombs** — clay pots filled with gunpowder, hurled from catapults
- **Rockets** — the first known rockets in history, used both as weapons and for signaling
- **The first true guns** — bronze tubes that fired projectiles using gunpowder, around 1200 CE

The Mongols, who conquered China in the 1200s, took gunpowder technology with them as they invaded westward. Muslim empires got it from the Mongols. Europeans got it from Muslims (probably through Spain and via Arab merchants) around **1250 CE**.

## Europe took gunpowder and made cannons

Europeans didn't invent gunpowder, but they industrialized cannon-making in ways that changed the world:

- **The end of medieval castles.** For a thousand years, a stone castle with high walls could stand off any army. Cannons made walls obsolete in one generation. This is one of the reasons feudalism collapsed in Europe — kings with cannons could crack any noble's castle.
- **The end of many independent kingdoms.** Small states couldn't afford cannon foundries. Big states could. Europe consolidated into a few big kingdoms as a direct result.
- **European ships with cannons.** In the 1400s and 1500s, Europeans put cannons on ships. This meant a European fleet could sail up to a port anywhere in the world and destroy it. Combined with the [compass](../compass/), this is what enabled European colonization of much of the globe.

## What humanity kept

Every modern firearm — every rifle, pistol, cannon, artillery piece — is a direct descendant of Song dynasty fire lances. Every rocket that has ever launched a satellite or gone to the moon uses the same basic principle (rapid combustion pushing something forward) that Chinese engineers worked out 1,000 years ago.

Also: fireworks. Which is fun. Chinese New Year fireworks are the original use, and still one of the best uses.

## The honest, complicated part

Gunpowder killed more people than probably any other invention in human history. **Tens of millions**, at least, over the centuries. It made possible European colonization of the Americas, Africa, and Asia — which killed enormous numbers of people through direct violence and disease. It made possible the World Wars of the 20th century, which killed over 100 million people combined.

**Was inventing gunpowder a good idea?** That's a genuinely hard question. It made human civilizations more powerful. It also made humans much more dangerous to each other.

The Chinese alchemists who invented it were looking for a way to live forever. They handed humanity a tool that made it much easier to die.

This is a pattern that keeps repeating in human history: our best minds, working on one problem, accidentally invent something that solves and creates enormous new problems. **Nuclear physics, the internet, AI — same shape.**

Something to think about.
