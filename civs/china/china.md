# China: Four Thousand Years, One Country

**East Asia · earliest civilizations ~2000 BCE · the Song dynasty golden age 960–1279 CE · still going**

Here is a fact your school books probably do not lead with: **China is the oldest country still around.** Not the oldest culture — that's a longer argument — but the oldest continuously running political and cultural tradition. Egypt is older but Egyptian civilization ended and was replaced. Sumer ended. Rome ended. China had emperors from **221 BCE to 1912 CE** — over 2,000 years — and even after the emperors ended, it was still China.

This page will not try to cover 4,000 years. It will cover **one moment** that most Westerners have never heard of, when China was so far ahead of the rest of the world that the gap did not close for 500 years.

## The Song Dynasty Golden Age (960–1279 CE)

While Europe was in the middle of what it calls the "Middle Ages" (fighting over castles, mostly illiterate, no plumbing), China under the **Song dynasty** was doing this:

- **Paper money.** China invented paper (around 100 CE, over a thousand years before it reached Europe). Under the Song, they invented **paper currency** — printed banknotes backed by the government. Europe did not have paper money for another 700 years.
- **The magnetic compass.** Invented in China around 200 BCE for feng shui, adapted for navigation by the Song around 1040 CE. Chinese sailors used compasses centuries before European sailors did.
- **Gunpowder.** Chinese alchemists in the 800s were looking for a potion to give immortality and accidentally invented an explosive powder instead. By the Song dynasty, they had rockets, flamethrowers, land mines, and bombs. Europe did not get gunpowder until the 1300s.
- **Movable-type printing.** A commoner named **Bi Sheng** invented movable clay type around 1040 CE — over 400 years before Gutenberg. (See our [Printing Press page](../../techs/printing-press/) for the whole story.)
- **The mechanical clock.** A civil servant named **Su Song** built a giant water-powered clock in 1092 CE with a chain drive and escapement mechanism — the same core mechanics European clockmakers would rediscover 200 years later.
- **Mass literacy.** The Song had a **civil service exam system** that let poor kids compete for government jobs based on knowledge, not birth. To pass, you had to be able to read and write. So schools popped up everywhere. **Twenty to thirty percent** of Song men could read. In Europe at the same time, reading was mostly limited to monks — under five percent.
- **Cities bigger than any in Europe.** The Song capital Kaifeng had **over a million people**. London at the time had maybe 40,000. Paris had about 100,000.

## Why isn't this in your school books

Two reasons.

**One:** Western history books were written mostly by Westerners, so they focused on Europe. When they talked about "the invention of X," they often meant "when Europeans figured out X." The Chinese versions came earlier but were treated as trivia, not the main story. This is slowly changing but has a long way to go.

**Two:** The Song eventually fell — to the Mongols in 1279 — and later Chinese dynasties (Ming, Qing) turned inward and stopped sharing much with the outside world. By the time Europeans started paying attention to China in the 1500s, the Song golden age was long over. Europeans saw a slower, more closed China and assumed that was what China had always been.

## What humanity kept from China

Almost everything. Take a look around the room you're in. If you see:

- **Paper** — Chinese, ~100 CE
- **A printed book** — Chinese moveable type, ~1040
- **Paper money** — Chinese, ~1000
- **A compass or GPS device** (all use magnetic direction as a base layer) — Chinese, ~200 BCE / adapted for navigation ~1040
- **Silk** (or any tightly woven fabric technique based on it) — Chinese, ~2700 BCE
- **Tea** — Chinese, first cultivated ~1500 BCE
- **Porcelain** (or "china") — Chinese, ~600 CE
- **Fireworks** — Chinese, ~900 CE
- **A wheelbarrow** — Chinese, ~100 CE (the West took 900 more years to figure it out)
- **A crossbow** in a museum — Chinese, ~500 BCE
- **A kite** — Chinese, ~500 BCE

That is one civilization, over one long stretch, giving the rest of us a huge chunk of what we use every day.

## The honest, complicated part

China's story is also, honestly, a story of enormous cruelty at times — mass executions, wars that killed tens of millions, systematic oppression of women (foot binding was a Chinese practice, and a very cruel one), and long stretches of dictatorship. The current government of China (the People's Republic, since 1949) has done things the world is still figuring out how to talk about, including a famine in the 1960s that killed tens of millions and ongoing suppression of ethnic minorities like Uyghurs and Tibetans.

A country as old as China contains everything a country can be: the highest and the lowest. Do not let anyone tell you it is all one thing.
