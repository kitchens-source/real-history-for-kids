# Egypt (and the African Kingdom That Ruled It)

**Northeast Africa · earliest kingdoms ~3100 BCE · Kushite pharaohs ruled Egypt ~747 to 656 BCE · ancient period ends ~30 BCE**

You already know about ancient Egypt. Pyramids, pharaohs, hieroglyphs, mummies, King Tut, Cleopatra. So this page is not going to tell you what you already know. It is going to tell you the parts your school books usually leave out.

## Egypt is in Africa

That sounds like a "duh." But most old history books drew a line — an imaginary line — between "ancient Egypt" (put in the same chapter as Rome and Greece) and "the rest of Africa" (skipped or put in a separate, later chapter). This lie is 200 years old and has done real damage. Ancient Egypt was an African civilization, connected by the Nile River to civilizations further south in Africa, trading and fighting and intermarrying with them for **thousands of years**.

The most important of those southern neighbors was called **Nubia**, and later, **the Kingdom of Kush**.

## The Kingdom of Kush

South of Egypt, along the Nile, in what is now Sudan, was a civilization that existed **as long as ancient Egypt did**. Kush had its own pharaohs, its own pyramids (way more pyramids than Egypt, actually — about 200 of them, still standing, mostly ignored by tourism), its own writing system, its own gods. For most of Egyptian history, Kush and Egypt were neighbors — sometimes at peace, sometimes at war, always connected.

Then something happened that the old history books really do not want to talk about:

## When Kush conquered Egypt

Around **747 BCE**, Egypt was a mess. Its central government had collapsed and it was split into competing pieces. A Kushite king named **Piye** (say it: "PEE-yay") looked north from his capital in Napata and decided to unite Egypt himself.

He marched his army north. He won every battle. He took the capital. He was crowned pharaoh of both Kush and Egypt.

For about **a hundred years — from roughly 747 to 656 BCE — Egypt was ruled by Black African pharaohs from Kush**, called the **25th Dynasty**. They restored the old Egyptian religion, rebuilt temples, revived the arts. Some Egyptologists argue this was one of the most peaceful and successful periods in late Egyptian history.

Their names: Piye, Shabaka, Shebitku, Taharqa, Tantamani. If you have never heard of them, that is on purpose. When 19th-century European Egyptologists were writing the "history of Egypt," they either skipped the Kushite pharaohs, or claimed they were actually somehow "not really African," or reduced them to a footnote.

The Kushite pharaohs are on the walls of every major Egyptian temple. They are in the archaeological record. They existed. School books just forgot to mention them.

## Some other things Egypt actually did

Because you already learned some of the standard stuff, here are some things you probably did not learn:

- **Written peace treaty.** The Egyptians and the Hittites (who invented iron working — see our [Iron Working page](../../techs/iron-working/)) signed the first known **peace treaty** in world history around 1259 BCE, after fighting each other to a standstill. Both copies survive. Fair, well-drafted, and it held. Countries have been trying and failing to write good peace treaties ever since.
- **Female pharaohs.** Not just Cleopatra (who was actually Greek, not Egyptian — she was the last ruler of a Greek dynasty that had ruled Egypt since Alexander the Great). Real Egyptian female pharaohs include **Hatshepsut** (1479–1458 BCE) who reigned as a full pharaoh for 20 years and is one of the most successful rulers Egypt ever had. Her stepson-nephew, Thutmose III, tried to erase her from history after her death. He did not fully succeed, and she's back in the record now.
- **Medicine.** The Edwin Smith Papyrus (an actual scroll you can go see) is a medical textbook from about 1600 BCE describing surgeries, brain injuries, and treatments. It is careful, empirical, and shockingly close to how a modern doctor would think about the same problems.
- **The 365-day calendar with 12 months.** That is Egyptian. So is dividing the day into 24 hours.

## The honest, complicated part

Ancient Egypt was built by huge numbers of workers. **Some** were slaves, but modern archaeology has strongly overturned the old idea that pyramids were built by slaves — the evidence points to well-paid, well-fed skilled Egyptian workers who took real pride in their jobs. Slavery existed and was ugly, but the pyramids specifically were more like a national jobs program.

Also: ancient Egypt lasted so long that "ancient Egyptian" covers vastly different peoples across three thousand years. Some pharaohs were locally Egyptian. Some were Kushite (Black African). Some were Persian (during periods of Persian rule). Cleopatra was Greek. Trying to make ancient Egypt "one thing racially" is a modern argument that ancient Egyptians would have found strange.

## What humanity kept from Egypt (and Kush)

**One:** So much of what became "Western civilization" was actually Egyptian first, transmitted through Greece. Greek mathematics and philosophy leaned heavily on Egyptian foundations. Plato studied in Egypt. Pythagoras studied in Egypt. So did Herodotus.

**Two:** The idea that a country can last a very long time. Egypt was a going concern for **3,000 years**. That is longer than everything since Egypt combined.

**Three:** The record. Because Egyptians wrote on stone and papyrus in a dry climate, we know their names — real individuals from 4,000 years ago — their families, their jobs, their loves, their complaints, their bad days. Most ancient people have vanished into anonymity. Egyptians did not.

**Four:** Sudan is right there. The direct descendants of ancient Kush live in what is now Sudan and Egypt. Their history did not end. Their story is ongoing.
