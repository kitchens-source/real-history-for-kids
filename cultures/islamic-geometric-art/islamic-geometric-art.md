# Islamic Geometric Art

**A visual tradition that started around the 8th century CE and is still alive today · Uses complex, mathematically precise patterns of interlocking shapes · Found on mosques, palaces, books, carpets, and everyday objects from Spain to Indonesia**

Walk into a great mosque anywhere from Cordoba to Isfahan to Istanbul, and look up. What you will see is one of the most breathtaking visual traditions in human history: **complex geometric patterns** that seem to go on forever, interlocking, unfolding, mathematically perfect.

These patterns are not decoration. They are a whole philosophy in visual form.

## Why geometric?

There is a common story that Islam "forbids" pictures of people or animals. That is only partly true — Islamic art actually includes many pictures of people (especially in Persian miniatures, see our [Persia page](../../civs/persia/)). What Islam does discourage is **pictures inside religious spaces**, and specifically pictures of God or the Prophet Muhammad. The reason: Islamic scholars worried that pictures would become **idols** — objects people would worship instead of God.

So Islamic artists working on religious buildings had to develop **a different visual language** — one that could be beautiful and meaningful without being representational.

They came up with three main approaches, often combined:

1. **Calligraphy** — the Arabic script written in beautiful ways, mostly quoting the Quran.
2. **Vegetal patterns** (the "arabesque") — abstracted plants, flowers, and vines.
3. **Geometric patterns** — the mathematically precise star-and-polygon designs that are the subject of this page.

## The mathematics

Islamic geometric patterns are built on **hidden mathematics** most Western art traditions never developed. Muslim artists working with mathematicians (see our [Mathematics page](../../techs/mathematics/) and [Arabia page](../../civs/arabia/)) figured out:

- **Symmetry groups.** How to create patterns that could tile a plane infinitely without ever repeating identically.
- **Star polygons.** Six-pointed, eight-pointed, twelve-pointed, sixteen-pointed stars interlocking with polygons around them, with every angle and length calculated precisely.
- **"Girih" tiles.** In the 1200s CE, Persian architects developed a set of five basic shapes that could be combined to create patterns of astonishing complexity. Modern mathematicians only fully understood the underlying math in the **2000s** — some patterns show **quasi-crystalline symmetry** that was not formally recognized in Western mathematics until Roger Penrose's work in 1974.
- **Meaning.** For the artists, the endless interlocking patterns were a visual metaphor for **infinity** — the idea that God's creation, like the pattern, extends forever in every direction, ordered but boundless.

## Where you can see it

- **The Alhambra, in Granada, Spain** (built 1238–1358, when Spain was part of the Muslim world). The most famous geometric-tiled palace in existence. Every surface is covered in patterns. It has inspired artists ever since, most famously M. C. Escher, who visited in 1922 and 1936 and said his later work owed everything to the Alhambra.
- **The Blue Mosque, Istanbul** (built 1609–1616). Named for the 20,000+ blue Iznik tiles covering the interior in geometric and vegetal patterns.
- **The Shah Mosque, Isfahan, Iran** (built 1611–1629). Considered one of the greatest achievements of Persian architecture. The interior domes and walls are covered in some of the most sophisticated geometric tilework ever made.
- **The Taj Mahal, India** (built 1631–1653). While it is best known for its shape, the details include exquisite geometric patterns inlaid in marble.
- **Books, carpets, ceramics, metalwork.** Every everyday Islamic artistic object of the medieval period.

## What humanity kept

**A whole tradition of "art without pictures"** that continues today. Modern designers, mathematicians, architects, and computer scientists all study Islamic geometric patterns. They influence:

- **Modern graphic design** and typography.
- **Architecture** — from Frank Lloyd Wright's textile-block houses to modern parametric architecture.
- **Video game visual design** and pattern generation algorithms.
- **Mathematical research** into symmetry, tiling, and quasi-crystals.

Also: the philosophy that **beauty can be built without representation** — that pattern, rhythm, and mathematics can carry the same emotional weight as painting a face or a landscape — is a genuinely Islamic contribution to how humans think about beauty.

## What we can learn

**Art can carry deep meaning without pictures of things.** Music does this. Architecture does this. Islamic geometric art proves that a whole visual tradition can be built this way and remain beautiful and moving for a thousand years.

**Constraint can be a source of creativity, not a limit.** Islamic artists working under a religious constraint (no religious imagery) responded not by shrinking their art but by inventing an entirely new mathematical-aesthetic tradition. Every artist working today under any constraint — commercial, technical, ethical — is walking a version of the same path.

**Different cultures can look at the same problem and solve it in genuinely different ways.** Christian art in Europe went representational (paintings of saints, Jesus, biblical scenes). Islamic art went geometric. Both are valid. Both are beautiful. **Human beauty is not one thing.**
